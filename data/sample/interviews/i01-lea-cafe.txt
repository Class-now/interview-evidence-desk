# i01 - Lea, owner of a 12-seat specialty cafe, 6 part-time staff (SYNTHETIC)
Interviewer: Thanks for making time. Can you walk me through how the rota gets made each week?
Lea: Sunday evening, kitchen table, a paper grid and my phone. Everyone sends me their availability on WhatsApp.
Interviewer: How long does that take?
Lea: The grid itself, maybe an hour and a half. It's the messages around it that eat the week.
Interviewer: Tell me about the last time something changed after you published it.
Lea: Last Tuesday Hugo texted at 6:40 in the morning that he had a fever, and I open at 7:30.
Lea: I called four people before Sarah picked up, and I opened alone for the first hour.
Interviewer: How often does that happen?
Lea: Two or three times a week there's some change. Sick, exams, a bus strike.
Lea: Honestly the Sunday grid is fine, it's the 6 a.m. phone calls that make me want to sell the place.
Interviewer: Have you tried any software for this?
Lea: I paid for one of those scheduling apps for three months, about 35 euros a month, then cancelled.
Interviewer: Why did you cancel?
Lea: It was built for restaurants with forty staff. Too many buttons, and my students never installed it.
Interviewer: If staff found their own replacement, would you need to approve it?
Lea: For the morning shift, yes, I need someone who can run the machine alone. Afternoons I don't care.
Lea: If two baristas swap, I just want to know by the evening before.
Interviewer: Anything else about this I should know?
Lea: I'd keep paying for something if it just fixed the morning panic. I don't need payroll from it.
