Decided by: **Demo founder** (human). Evidence prepared by Interview Evidence Desk from 8 interviews; 4 model-proposed quotes were rejected because they did not appear in the transcripts.

## H1: Independent cafe and bakery owners spend 3 or more hours a week building and fixing staff rotas.
- **Decision: REJECT** - Only 3 of 8 reach 3 hours; Tomas, Claire, Ravi and Sofia are at 10 min to 2 h. Building the rota is not the pain.
- Evidence lean: mixed (for 7 / against 9, weights said=1 did=2 paid=3; 8/8 interviews)
- Devil's advocate: While the evidence leans against the claim, the interviews suggest that the time spent building and fixing staff rotas can vary significantly. Some owners, like [i02-marc-bakery] and [i05-amina-tea], spend up to four or five hours a week, but others, such as [i04-tomas-brunch] and [i07-claire-crepe], spend only an hour or less. This variability indicates that the claim of spending 3 or more hours a week is not universally true.
- Would change our mind: The owner should ask interviewees to provide specific examples of how much time they spend on rota building and fixing, and to quantify it in a spreadsheet or a time-tracking tool to better understand the actual time spent.
- For: "Every Thursday, it takes her most of the afternoon, three to four hours." (i02-marc-bakery, line 2, did)
- For: "Two shops means two rotas, and the bakers start at 4 a.m., so the constraints are tight." (i02-marc-bakery, line 3, did)
- Against: "Building it, about an hour a week now, the app copies last week." (i04-tomas-brunch, line 4, did)
- Against: "The summer rota takes me maybe two hours a week." (i07-claire-crepe, line 2, did)

## H2: Last-minute changes (sick calls, swaps, no-shows) hurt more than building the weekly rota.
- **Decision: ACCEPT** - Real losses already happened: Marc lost bread sales, Ravi refunded 230 EUR, Jonas lost a shift lead. Some quotes the agent put here are really about H4.
- Evidence lean: supported (for 8 / against 0, weights said=1 did=2 paid=3; 7/8 interviews)
- Devil's advocate: While the evidence suggests that last-minute changes do cause significant pain, it's important to consider that the current rota system is designed to handle these situations. If the rota is well-constructed and flexible, it can adapt to unexpected changes without causing as much disruption as the interviews suggest.
- Would change our mind: In the next interviews, I would ask owners to provide examples of how their current rota system has successfully handled last-minute changes without causing significant disruption.
- For: "I spent my whole break on the phone." (i05-amina-tea, line 7, did)
- For: "A baker didn't show up in October. No message. We lost the morning bread for the second shop." (i02-marc-bakery, line 5, said)

## H3: Owners will pay 20 EUR or more per month for a tool that handles shift swaps automatically.
- **Decision: DOUBT** - Three people already pay 35-89 EUR (see the money lines the model missed), but the small shops refuse. Price may depend on size, not on swaps.
- Evidence lean: mixed (for 3 / against 4, weights said=1 did=2 paid=3; 7/8 interviews)
- Devil's advocate: Despite the willingness expressed by some interviewees to pay more than 20 EUR per month for an automatic shift swap tool, the evidence is mixed and weakened by the clear indication from others that they would not pay even 15 EUR per month for such a service. The strong willingness to pay from some does not outweigh the strong counterarguments from others.
- Would change our mind: Owners should ask interviewees about their willingness to pay for other features of the tool, such as automated payroll or inventory management, to better understand their overall willingness to pay for the service.
- For: "If it checked the labour rules automatically, I would pay more than 20 euros, that's not the issue." (i02-marc-bakery, line 15, said)
- For: "I'd pay 30 a month without thinking if it keeps my next shift lead from burning out." (i06-jonas-roastery, line 11, said)
- Against: "No. I would not pay a monthly fee for three people, even five euros feels like too much." (i03-sofia-kiosk, line 9, said)
- Against: "I already pay for the big one. I won't add another subscription just for swaps." (i04-tomas-brunch, line 11, said)

## H4: Owners are willing to let staff swap shifts among themselves without approving each swap.
- **Decision: REJECT** - Most want a check, not freedom: Marc, Claire, Lea mornings, Ravi wants both sides to confirm. Several 'for' quotes are about price, not approval.
- Evidence lean: supported (for 7 / against 1, weights said=1 did=2 paid=3; 6/8 interviews)
- Devil's advocate: Despite the willingness expressed by some staff to swap shifts without approval, the evidence is weak and inconsistent. The owner's counter-argument about trust and the need for approval suggests that the system is still not fully trusted, even when staff are willing to do it themselves.
- Would change our mind: Owners should show examples of approved shift swaps to demonstrate the process and build trust with staff.
- For: "They already do. They tell me after, and that's fine, I trust them completely." (i03-sofia-kiosk, line 11, did)
- For: "Ines handles it. Last month she worked eleven days in a row covering gaps, and then she quit." (i06-jonas-roastery, line 6, did)
- Against: "The issue is my wife trusting it. She has tried two apps and gone back to the spreadsheet both times." (i02-marc-bakery, line 16, said)
