- "Ines handles it. Last month she worked eleven days in a row covering gaps, and then she quit." - i06-jonas-roastery L6, workaround, said - why: The quote indicates that Ines worked eleven days in a row to cover gaps, which suggests that the cafe and bakery likely spend a significant amount of time managing staff rotas. However, it does not specify the exact number of hours Jonas or the cafe spends on this task. While it implies that a substantial amount of time is spent, it does not confirm that it is 3 or more hours a week.
**Against**

- "I export last week from the till, adjust it in a spreadsheet, about two hours on Sunday." - i08-ravi-donuts L2, counter, did - why: Ravi states that he spends about two hours on Sunday adjusting the rota. This is less than the three or more hours mentioned in the hypothesis.
- "The grid itself, maybe an hour and a half." - i01-lea-cafe L4, counter, said - why: Lea states that the grid itself takes 'maybe an hour and a half,' which is less than the 3 hours mentioned in the hypothesis. This suggests that the time spent on the grid itself is not 3 or more hours, but rather less.
- "It's not a problem at all." - i03-sofia-kiosk L3, counter, said - why: Sofia states that organizing the rota is not a problem and takes only ten minutes, which is less than the threshold of 3 hours per week.
- "The rota is the same every week, I change it maybe once a month." - i03-sofia-kiosk L2, counter, said - why: Sofia states that the rota is the same every week and she changes it maybe once a month. This suggests that she does not spend a significant amount of time (more than 3 hours a week) on building and fixing staff rotas.
- "I would pay around 15 euros a month. Twenty-five would be too much for us this year." - i05-amina-tea L13, counter, said - why: Amina states that she would pay around 15 euros a month for a better system, which is less than 25 euros. This suggests that she does not currently spend 25 euros or more on staff rotas, and thus does not spend 3 or more hours a week on this task. Therefore, her statement is against the hypothesis.
- "In summer it's chaos, in winter it's me and one person. The summer rota takes me maybe two hours a week." - i07-claire-crepe L2, counter, said - why: Claire states that the summer rota takes her 'maybe two hours a week.' This is less than the 3 or more hours required by the hypothesis.

> Devil's advocate: The current evidence is mixed, with some owners spending significant time and others spending minimal time, making it difficult to generalize. More consistent data from a larger sample size would be needed to confirm the hypothesis.
> Would change our mind: If two more owners provided detailed weekly logs showing they spend 3 or more hours each week on staff rotas, it would significantly strengthen the hypothesis.

This supports the hypothesis that last-minute changes hurt more than building the weekly rota.

This supports the hypothesis that last-minute changes can be time-consuming and disruptive, potentially hurting the process of building the weekly rota.
- "Last Friday we refunded 14 orders, around 230 euros, because two riders both thought the other was coming." - i08-ravi-donuts L5, pain, did - why: The evidence mentions that 14 orders were refunded due to a misunderstanding between two riders.
